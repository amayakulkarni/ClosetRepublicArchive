# Wiki content sources

A list of known wikis, archives, forks and reference sources that could feed https://wiki.closetrepublic.com, with what each one allows.

Researched 2026-10-20. Licences change: the importer (`importer/`) reads each MediaWiki site's licence live at import time and refuses to import if it is not on the allowed list. For every non-MediaWiki source, re-check the licence by hand before using it.

This is research, not legal advice. Before relying on the "Reuse" column for anything beyond linking, read the source's own licence page.

## How to read this list

| Tier | Meaning | What the wiki may do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A: Import** | Open licence that allows copying and changing, compatible with CC BY-SA 4.0 | Copy text through the importer, with attribution and licence notice, then fact-check before publishing |
| **B: Facts and metadata** | Public law, CC0 data, or government text that may be reproduced | Quote or summarise with a citation; reproduce statutes and judgments from the official source |
| **C: Link and cite** | All rights reserved, unclear, or per-item rights | Write original text, cite and link the source. Copy nothing. Ask the owner for written permission to reuse anything |
| **X: Do not use** | Terms forbid reuse, or the content is about identifiable private people | Do not import; link only if useful |

"Web address not confirmed" means the organisation is real but its official site was not confirmed during research; confirm it before linking.

"Verified" means the licence or terms were read on 2026-10-20. "Check at import" means the licence could not be read from here and must be confirmed (the importer does this automatically for MediaWiki sites).

## Licence for the Closet Republic wiki itself

Set the wiki's licence to **CC BY-SA 4.0** (Administration > General > Site Info > License). Imported CC BY-SA text requires that adapted pages stay under CC BY-SA, and CC BY-SA 3.0 text may be adapted under 4.0. Text under NC (non-commercial) or ND (no derivatives) licences is not compatible and must never be imported.

## 3. India: law and government (primary sources)

Indian law allows reproducing Acts and judgments (Copyright Act 1957, section 52(1)(q)), but not the headnotes and editorial additions of law reporters. Government websites following the GIGW guidelines usually allow free reproduction if the text is reproduced accurately, the source is acknowledged, and it is not used in a derogatory or misleading way. Third-party material on those sites is excluded.

| Source | What it has | Reuse | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in) | Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019; Rules 2020; Special Marriage Act; IPC/BNS text | Official Acts: reproducible with citation | B |
| [Supreme Court of India judgments](https://www.sci.gov.in) | NALSA v. Union of India (2014), Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018), Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017, privacy), Supriyo @ Supriya Chakraborty v. Union of India (2023) | Judgment text reproducible; cite case name, date and citation | B |
| [eCourts / High Court sites](https://ecourts.gov.in) | High Court rulings, for example Madras High Court in Arunkumar (2019) and S. Sushma (2021, guidelines including on conversion "therapy") | As above | B |
| National Portal for Transgender Persons (Ministry of Social Justice; web address not confirmed) | Certificate and ID card process under the 2019 Act | Government site: summarise and link; reproduce only with acknowledgement | B |
| [National Medical Commission](https://www.nmc.org.in) | 2022 notice treating conversion "therapy" as professional misconduct | Government body: cite and link | B |
| [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org) | Searchable judgments | The judgments are public, but the site's own terms and formatting are not. **Do not scrape.** Use it to find cases, then copy from the official court source | C |

## Suggested first imports

Start small so reviewers are not flooded (the importer imports at most 25 pages per run by default):

1. Terminology: core orientation and gender terms from one LGBTA Wiki fork, if its licence passes the check.
2. India law explainers, written fresh from Tier B primary sources: NALSA 2014, Navtej Johar 2018, the 2019 Act and 2020 Rules, the 2023 marriage-equality judgment.
3. English Wikipedia: "LGBTQ rights in India", "Hijra", "Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019", "Section 377", "Kinnar Akhada" as drafts for rewriting.
4. A "Get help in India" resource list written fresh, linking Tier C organisations, each checked for a working contact before publishing.
